Answers you gave for Beta 0.9.1

QuestionYour answerHow it works in the game
Which bombs, and how are they used?4 kindsSmall (thrown, bounces), fire (thrown, bursts on impact), sticky (thrown, sticks), big (placed at your feet). All break blocks; your own bombs hurt you and your side at half strength.
What do rubble and burnt blocks become?Rubble that fadesRubble blocks land from the blast and crumble away after 3 days; burnt blocks (scorched stone, ash, charred wood) stay until mined.
When does the Demolitionist arrive?After a bossOnce the Goblin Beastmaster (the first boss) is beaten, with a new house in your village.
How do shop upgrades work?3 levels, coins and materialsTalk to the shopkeeper. Level 2: 400 coins, 40 stone bricks, 10 iron bars, needs a Town. Level 3: 1,200 coins, 60 stone bricks, 10 gold bars, 2 diamonds, needs a City.

Assumptions for Beta 0.9.1 (change any of these)

  • Bombs never break blocks in your own claimed village or your own castles, so a stray bomb can't wreck your home; they do break enemy castles.
  • Chests, portals, furnaces, workbenches, thrones, trophies, the dragon's gold and unbreakable blocks survive any blast; a door is blown out whole, both halves.
  • Only small and fire bombs (and black powder) can be crafted; big and sticky bombs are the Demolitionist's.
  • About 40% of the blocks a blast breaks drop their item; up to 14 rubble blocks land per blast.
  • Your own village has the Jeweler (Town), War engineer (City) and Demolitionist as shopkeepers; the Blacksmith, Cook and Shipwright are in the villages you conquer (or any villager you give that job). Shop levels count for each village separately.
  • In older worlds every shop starts at the level the village already had, so nothing you could buy before disappears.
  • In multiplayer a guest's bomb breaks blocks for everyone, but rubble only lands from the host's bombs.

Assumptions for Beta 0.9.0 (change any of these)

  • The prepped farm has 16 crop plots (wheat, carrots, potatoes and pumpkins, some ripe and some growing) around a water block, a fenced pen (fence along the ground, taller posts at each end, no gate) with 2 cows, 2 sheep, a boar and a rabbit tamed inside and a trough chest beside it, and a farmhouse with a bed, workbench, furnace and a chest of seeds, food, fences and torches.
  • The farm is built on the first open, fairly flat ground 20 or more blocks to the right of where you start (or the left if there is none); trees there are cleared and the ground is leveled.
  • Forced weather stays until you pick Natural, even across days; the strength setting changes the world's weather setting.
  • "Skip to next morning" counts as a new day: pens produce, crops grow and the weather changes (unless forced).
  • Raw pumpkins are no longer eaten; clicking with one places it. Pumpkin pie still heals.
  • Hawks tame 3 times in 5 and need 1 piece of meat; eagles need 2; small birds tame 4 times in 5 with 1 wheat seed.
  • Plants under water stop growing until the water dries. Water knocks loose torches, lanterns, lamps and campfires (you get them back as items); a jack-o'-lantern is a solid block, so water does not reach inside it.
  • In the cold, plants die a few at a time over about a minute near you, and only near where you are. Rain over an ice island falls as snow, and ice islands slowly gather snow even on clear days. Snow on ice islands never melts. Plants under leaves are not under a roof. Regrowing plants are tall grass, ferns and flowers, and never closer than 2 blocks to another plant.
  • Farm tab recipes (by hand): 2 wheat seeds from 1 wheat, 2 carrot seeds from 1 carrot, 2 seed potatoes from 1 potato, 4 pumpkin seeds from 1 pumpkin; sapling = apple + dirt; 2 tall grass = wheat seed + dirt; fern = wheat seed + dirt; bush = 2 wheat seeds + dirt; red flower = wheat seed + berries, yellow = wheat seed + honey, blue = wheat seed + raw fish; berry bush = 3 berries + dirt; herb plant = 2 herbs + dirt; sugar cane = 2 wheat seeds + sand; cactus = wheat seed + 3 sand; glow mushroom = 2 mushrooms.
  • Door ramps: the first block outside the door sits at floor level, then the ground rises or falls one block per block until it meets the natural ground (at least 5 blocks, at most 10). Trees in the way are removed. A ramp stops at another building. Houses that were already standing are left as they are.
  • Castles: each floor of the sky tower is climbed through a 4-block gap with two one-way ledges under it, and floors alternate sides. Magma rooms join by doorways (3 high) or 3-block holes with ledges; two extra doorways make small loops. The magma maze layout is random for each world. The throne room unlocks like any other room. Loot chests that were inside the old castles are gone when they are rebuilt. The boss still waits on his throne until you enter his chamber.
  • The sky realm is 96 rows tall in new worlds (72 before). Older worlds keep 72 rows, because moving a whole world down would break buildings and saves; their sky tower is rebuilt shorter instead.
  • Taming reach is measured from the edge of the animal (3 blocks, 4.5 for flying birds), so big animals can be tamed from a little farther from their middle. A lantern counts as attached when a solid block is above it or beside it, when it is in front of a back wall, or when it stands on a built (not natural) block. Village lanterns hang at head height beside the house doors.
  • Village levelling: if two neighbouring buildings have floors at different heights, each gets 3 flat blocks and the ground between slopes evenly. Fences and crops count as part of the village and are not dug up; ramps stop at them. An end ramp is at most 13 blocks long, so a very tall hill or deep drop may still leave a step farther out.
  • Grappling hooks: winding up for a full second throws the full distance; a quick tap throws about a third. The hook grabs any solid block or platform but not water. Hooks are found in about 1 chest in 17 (better hooks in richer chests). On a computer the hook key is X, since G already switches flying.
  • Miners: an enemy hit leaves a miner with at least 1 health and sends him to the village to rest for 30 seconds before going back down.
  • The grappling hook falls as it flies, so aim a little above what you want to grab, as with fishing. Holding longer throws it harder and farther.
  • Lava already lying in the world stays still until something next to it changes (a block dug out, a dam broken); then it flows. Lava cools to obsidian where it touches water when it is at least half deep, otherwise to stone. Coins dropped into lava are kept; items are destroyed.
  • Capture: soldiers on a voyage, scouts and anyone off the island are left out of the count. In older worlds the island count starts from the largest number the faction had before.
  • Grappling hook: ground at or below your feet counts as the ground (the hook lies there); a wall, a ceiling, or the top of anything higher than your feet counts as something to grab. If you walk too far from a hook lying on the ground, it is reeled in by itself.
  • Lava pools: about one underground pool for every 140 columns of world and one surface pool for every 700; surface pools are kept away from villages, castles, camps and water. Swimming in lava still burns you unless your armor protects you.
  • Lava: jump while your head is above the lava to spring out onto the shore. Blocks can be placed into lava and over it to make a bridge; wooden ones beside lava still burn, so use stone.
  • Rune levels (Kinard asked that runes not be overpowered and get harder to upgrade): levels I to V; level I is weak and level V equals the old full strength; reaching level II takes 2 more runes, III 3, IV 4 and V 5, so 15 runes for the top. A different rune resets the weapon to level I. Old saves: runes count as level I.
  • Class balance (from a full bot playthrough of every class): daggers lowered about 18%, archer bow bonus raised from 20% to 30%. The Magma Knight's keep and Vorthax for melee classes are flagged for review in the assessment doc.
  • Armies (Kinard asked that the dragon need all 20 soldiers and that enemy outposts be too hard alone): your damage to Vorthax is (most fighters you brought into the lair / 20)^2.5 of normal, at least 6%; it heals 60 x (1 - that share) health a second, so none with all 20; it lands more often and for longer with a bigger army; melee blows on it (yours and your soldiers') do double damage; about 30% of its attacks target a soldier. Measured with every class: alone and with 10 soldiers all five lost; with 15 only the archer won; with 20 all five won (mace 132 s, archer 177 s, knight 271 s, wizard 698 s, Beastmaster 854 s).
  • Enemy villages, their islands and outposts: with fewer than 5 of your fighters within 16 blocks, the whole garrison within 45 blocks rallies once you are within 25 blocks of a defender, hits you up to 5x as hard and takes as little as 25% of your damage (fading evenly from 0 to 5 fighters); dying there heals the garrison to full. Measured with every class alone: the knight, archer and mace failed in 10 minutes; the Beastmaster won only after 7 deaths and the wizard after 27. With 5 soldiers the knight, archer, mace and Beastmaster won in 18 to 46 seconds; the wizard, with the least health, needs about 10 (won in 23 seconds).
  • Thrown weapons, second round (Kinard asked): the mace flies where you aim when you let go; thrown weapons are drawn at their held size; reach +30% (sword about 9 blocks, mace chain 8); the Adamantite and Kraken daggers are thrown like small boomerangs, one every 0.22 s, at most 3 in the air, 0.9x damage per hit. The numbers in the next line are the first round.
  • Thrown weapons (decided with Kinard): only the top tier changes: the Adamantite sword and the Magma blade are always thrown like a boomerang (about 7 blocks out, hitting each enemy once going out and once coming back, 1.35x damage per hit); the Adamantite mace is on a 6-block chain (tap: throw, hitting each enemy once; hold: spin above your head, about 2.75 blocks across, each enemy hit at most every 0.35 s for 80% damage; let go: throw). The chain throw keeps the mace's normal 0.7 s recovery.
  • Multiplayer review: the player who attacks deals the damage; the others only see a replay of it. Your thrown sword or chain is sent with your position about ten times a second. In multiplayer the dragon's health is shared by taking the lowest value any player has, so its healing can be cut short when several players fight it; your own damage still depends on your own army in the lair.
  • Doors and water: tapping an open door that water is flowing through closes it, and the water in the doorway is pushed out (it is gone). Doors with water in them never close by themselves; you have to tap them.
  • Tree logs drop as pickups from the fallen tree's rubble (pieces chosen at random); in multiplayer a guest still gets them straight into the bag.
  • Grappling hook: jump lets go of a hold and jumps; letting go with the hook key at a ledge climbs it over about a third of a second.
  • Buckets (my assumptions): a bucket costs 3 iron bars at a workbench. It carries one block of water at a time. Pouring it on lava makes obsidian where the lava is deep (4 levels or more) and stone where it is shallow. There is no lava bucket: an empty bucket can't scoop lava.
  • Phones: the grappling hook is a small aiming joystick (decided by Kinard) rather than a button: touch to wind up, drag to aim, let go to throw; a quick tap throws up and forward. It sits just left of the right stick. The game asks for full screen on your first tap (some phones, like iPhones in Safari, only allow this from the home screen: use Share > Add to Home Screen).
  • Playing inside the Claude app: the bar at the top with the game name and the dark strips at the sides belong to the Claude app (they keep clear of the phone camera notch), so the game cannot cover them. For a truly full screen, open the game link in the phone browser, or save the game file and add it to your home screen.
  • Hills, cave entrances and wild pumpkin patches come with new worlds; old worlds get slopes, mist clouds and pumpkin patches when they load.

Assumptions for Beta 0.6.0 (change any of these)

  • Seasons last 6 days each; the sea is frozen for all of winter, and boats cannot be launched onto the ice.
  • Village tiers hold 20, 30 and 45 soldiers (reserves and expeditions count). Freed prisoners, tribute soldiers and rescued captives can go over the limit; hiring cannot.
  • Loyalty starts at 55, rises 5 on a day you visit (within 60 blocks), falls 3 on a day you don't, and gifts add 8 for 100 coins. Loyal is 70+. Below 25 there is a 50% chance each morning of rebellion; rebels left alive for 2 days free the village.
  • Each weapon line holds one rune; setting a different one replaces (destroys) the old one and starts again at level I.
  • Soldiers rank up at 5, 15 and 35 kills, each rank +10% health and damage. Traits start at Veteran.
  • Only your own kills count toward the bestiary, and only monsters and sea creatures have entries.
  • Expeditions run on game time, so they continue while you play elsewhere in the same world, but not while the game is closed.
  • Each boss trophy drops once until you mount it; it only works inside your home village.
  • Faces (Kinard asked to fix the eyes): every face uses one eye drawing. An eye colour too close to the skin is drawn darker so it shows; on the darkest skin the eye gets a white glint. Saved characters keep the colour they picked; only how it is drawn changed.
  • Boss ladder (Kinard asked for the bosses in order with various difficulties, each needing a handful of soldiers): the order is the one the game's own playthrough test uses: Goblin Beastmaster, Archmage, Magma Wizard, Sky Ranger, Magma Knight, Grukk, Kraken, Vorthax. Soldiers needed: 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 20. Soldiers count within 20 blocks of the boss (30 for the Kraken, where soldiers aboard your ship count); tamed animals count unless they are penned; other players and their soldiers count in a shared world. Below the number, your own damage is scaled from 15% (alone) up to full, and the boss heals up to 1.2% a second while it was hit in the last 20 seconds. Your soldiers' damage is never reduced.
  • The "sky wizard" is taken to be the Archmage of the wizard tower (whose defeat opens the sky realm). The flying wizards land every 9 to 12 seconds for 2.5 seconds so melee classes get their chance; they give up the chase 60 blocks from home.
  • Beastmaster stealth: every 7 to 10 seconds (5 to 7 when enraged), 2 seconds unseen and unhurtable, then a backstab for 2.2x damage if he is still next to his target.
  • Sky Ranger: gale every 11 s (8 enraged) when someone is within 12 blocks, 15 damage and a strong push; marked shot every 9 s (6 enraged), 70 damage, half the time aimed at you. Magma Knight: molten armor every 13 s (10 enraged) for 6 s, 20% damage taken, broken by 4 different attackers, then 150% damage for 4 s; eruptions every 9 s (6 enraged), 8 to 10 bursts of fire 26 damage each.
  • Trophies (Kinard asked for 2 by 2 replicas on shelves): a trophy fills 2 by 2 blocks, its lower left corner on the left of two trophy shelves side by side. Shelves need a back wall behind them and are crafted 2 at a time from 4 planks and an iron bar. Taking a shelf down drops the trophy. The replica is drawn from the boss's own sprite.
  • Patrol ships cost the faction 100 gold, come at most about once a day per faction, and only while you are on the open sea near their island.
  • A guest who sees no change from the host for 45 seconds takes over the world; a 12-second notice comes first.

Assumptions (change any of these)

  • Jumping to a friend from the map or their arrow is free and takes your party with you.
  • You can hold up to 3 villager tasks at once.
  • Taming with the whistle (and food) or as a Beastmaster always succeeds.
  • An enemy outpost or keep is captured once all of its guards are defeated.
  • Each realm boss drops two permanent upgrades, chosen from what you are missing: Swiftness III, Sprint III, Heart of vigor, Heart of the arcane.
  • The wider magma realm only appears in newly created worlds; older worlds keep their realm but get the bigger ore veins and the cloud stepping stones.
  • In multiplayer, a guest receives the host's live world when the host can save shared data for the game link; otherwise the guest builds the same world from its seed.
  • Built in Beta 0.6.0: enemies targeting guests' soldiers and pets, doors made from other woods, guests saving the host's world, enemy war parties travelling by boat, and enemy workers carrying goods home.
