Other rules worth knowing

  • Choosing posts: in Buildings, every post of every building has its own picker, named by floor and side (for example Post 5: roof, west). Pick a soldier from your party for each post, or Empty to call one back. Soldiers following you who get stuck in a cave or fall far behind catch up to a free spot beside you after a few seconds.
  • Multiplayer: everyone sees each other's attacks (the swing, its direction and the weapon held). Villagers are shared: when a guest hires a miner, the host's village gets him and every player sees the same one; whether the village is claimed and which castles are conquered are shared too. Players who join get the host's starting items (such as the recall mirror) and death rule.
  • Watching commanders: in the Army menu (Opposing villages), press Watch beside a commander and the camera follows him wherever he goes; the bar at the top shows what he is doing and carrying. Press Stop watching or Esc to come back. Blocks a commander digs or chops go into his own bag, not onto the ground.
  • Your miners dig real tunnels: from the bottom of the mine shaft they cut sideways, block by block, and carry what they dig up to the hut chest. When a tunnel gets long they start a new one a little deeper. The tunnels show on the map as they are dug - for every player in multiplayer too.
  • Faction commanders: each enemy castle's warlord plays the game much like you. Near you, he walks out past his walls to dig down for stone and ore or cut down trees; far away, he keeps gathering. With enough ore and wood he forges better gear - wood, iron, gold, diamond, mythril, adamantite - gaining health, armor and damage and carrying a matching sword (iron in about half a day, gold in about three days, adamantite in about a week). Every day or two he sails to another island to explore and loot a chest, and he leads his faction's war parties himself.
  • Defeating a commander costs his faction half its gold and one gear tier; a new commander rises 2 days later, again and again, until you capture his castle. One enemy faction always lives to the west of your home island and the other to the east, never side by side. No war party or raid comes for your island before day 3. Every attack is paid for and staffed by the faction itself: a raiding party (2-3 soldiers) costs 120 gold and 30 food, a war party (up to 10 soldiers, led by the commander) costs 360 gold and 40 food, and both are made of soldiers who leave the castle garrison - posted defenders stay, and at least 6 soldiers always remain at home. A faction short of gold, food or soldiers cannot attack, and a castle is weaker while its army is away. Survivors march home and rejoin the garrison. To claim a new island a faction must first defeat every enemy on it: it sends an expedition of 7 to 10 soldiers, and once the island is clear it conquers it by building an outpost near each end and a keep in the middle, all with garrisons (an expedition can also be beaten and driven off). You conquer a free island the same way: build a stone watchtower near each end and a stone keep in the middle; after that, enemy factions can no longer claim it. The Army menu (Opposing villages) and the Map screen show each commander's gear and what he is doing.
  • Miners: each miner brings 1 to 3 items a day to the hut chest. Every boss or mini-boss you defeat (the Archmage, Magma Wizard, Goblin Beastmaster, Grukk, warlords, Sky Ranger, Magma Knight, the Kraken and the dragon) makes every miner bring 3 to 5 more per day, up to 30 each. Once a miner has brought his share for the day, he rests until morning.
  • Animal pens (Buildings): a fenced yard with a gate and a trough chest (18 planks, 8 sticks, a door and a chest). Stand near your tamed farm animals and choose Put animals in; up to 8 live there. Each morning they fill the trough - cows give beef, sheep wool and mutton, boars and deer meat, rabbits carrots, birds seeds - and pairs of the same kind sometimes have young. Let them out any time.
  • Buildings have their own window: the house icon in the top bar, or Buildings and defense posts in the Army menu. Crafting has a Show row to list all recipes, only what you can craft now, or recipes by hand, at the workbench, furnace, blast furnace or workshop.
  • The eye button opens a small panel: hide or show your items, and tick what should stay visible while hidden (hotbar, bag, craft, army, buildings, mini-map, full screen, bars, info). H still toggles straight away. Hover over any button for a label saying what it does; the god menu and fly buttons have their own icons.
  • Settings has separate music and sound effects volume sliders. You can walk (A, D, W or the arrows) while a menu is open, and bag, shop, chest, army, recruit and map screens close once you walk 10 blocks away. You can attack while sprinting with Shift; Alt + click places a torch.
  • Glass: glass blocks (2 sand at a furnace) are solid - nobody can walk through them - but arrows, bolts and sight pass straight through. Your watchtowers and keep have 3-block-tall glass windows on both sides of every floor, and soldiers stationed there stand right beside the windows or at the roof edges, where they shoot best.
  • The stone keep's portal is on its roof. Chests are 2 blocks wide and cupboards (dressers) 2 blocks tall; furnaces, benches and chests in castles move over a block when a wall is in the way, so every one fits.
  • Enemies that cannot reach you no longer jump in place: they head to the nearest door and wait there. Faction soldiers open their own village's doors (never yours), and new faction recruits appear inside the castle, beside their shipwright or just outside the walls. Animals never spawn on castle roofs or other buildings.
  • Menus never freeze the world: the bag, crafting, buildings, army, map, chests, god menu and talking to villagers all keep the game running (only the pause menu pauses, in single player). Your hunger is full again after you respawn.
  • Villager jobs you change are permanent (saved with the village or keep) and, in multiplayer, every player sees the change. Items dropped in the host's world near other players appear for them too, and whoever picks one up takes it for everyone. Soldiers stationed at your posts never leave them, even when attacked.
  • Held posts: defenders in enemy watchtowers, outposts, the wizard tower, the magma fortress and on castle roofs stand at the window slits and battlements and keep shooting from there. They only leave their post once something hurts them. Soldiers you station at your own buildings snap straight to their post (beside a window slit or on the roof) and hold it the same way.
  • Castle roofs are flat and clear for 5 blocks either side of the middle, where the portal appears; the parapet and roof guards are on the outer parts.
  • Multiplayer: buildings put up by any player, soldiers stationed at posts, held defenders and faction changes are all shared, so every player sees the same world. Other players show on the mini-map (blue dots) and the full map (with their names), and when they are off-screen an arrow at the edge of your screen points to them with their name and distance in blocks.
  • Buildings (Craft → Buildings): build a wooden hut (8 logs, 12 planks, a door, a torch), a battlement wall (30 stone), a stone watchtower (70 stone, 8 planks, a door, 3 torches) or a stone keep (160 stone, 20 planks, a door, 6 torches). Choose one, then tap or click the ground within 40 blocks; Esc cancels. The outline turns red if anything other than trees, grass or plants is in the way (trees are cleared whole). Floors come with step ledges so you can climb them, roofs have one stone at each end, and the hut, watchtower and keep have a door on both sides (the hut is built from solid planks). If you build on top of yourself or your soldiers, they are moved to the nearest open space. The stone keep has a portal on its ground floor that joins your travel network, and its own quartermaster (troop upgrades), recruiter (hire soldiers) and beast trainer (animal upgrades).
  • Defense posts: each building has posts (hut 1, wall 2, tower 4, keep 6). In Craft → Buildings, station soldiers from your party at a building's posts; they stand guard there and fight anything that comes near. Recall them any time. Stationed soldiers are saved with the world.
  • Enemy castles have a parapet with slits on the hall roof, with archers and mages standing guard behind it. They stay at their posts and shoot at you.
  • Spectator mode (test world, God menu): enemies ignore you and cannot hurt you, so you can watch castles, factions and creatures act on their own. Free crafting (test world, God menu): every recipe, building and shop item is unlocked with no materials, stations or coins, and the All items tab appears in Craft.
  • Mini-map: the + and - in its corner make the mini-map bigger or smaller (4 sizes). Pressing - at the smallest size hides it, and a map button appears in the icon bar to bring it back. The M key always opens the full map.
  • Hunting: enemy faction soldiers no longer give up when you duck behind a wall. They work out a route - walking, climbing up to 2 blocks, and dropping down - to reach you, or the spot where they last saw you.
  • Multiplayer: everything the enemy factions change in the world - new castle levels, watchtowers, outposts and their mine shafts is sent to every player, so everyone sees the same world. Players who join later catch up on these changes.
  • XP, levels and the skill tree are switched off for now; boots, charms and other gear work as soon as you find them.
  • Army menu: at the bottom, Opposing villages shows each enemy village's food, gold, workers and army strength (soldiers at home, in outposts and marching). Villages grow food from farms and workers; their soldiers eat it, and a village out of food cannot recruit.
  • Screen: the buttons are icons - backpack (Bag), hammer (Craft), swords (Army), eye (Hide items), gear (Menu) and full screen. A live mini-map in the corner shows the area around you and other players; click it to open the map. The small arrow tab minimizes the icons and mini-map, and brings them back.
  • Music keeps playing in the map and all menus.
  • Allies: in the wizard tower the sky knights fight alongside the Archmage and his mages, and in the magma fortress the magma knights fight alongside the imps and the Magma Wizard.
  • Every island keeps to its own creatures: goblins live only in the goblin forest, frost archers and brutes only on ice islands, orcs and ogres on the orc island, and zombies, skeletons and ogres elsewhere.
  • Living factions: each enemy faction runs its own economy. Its workers chop wood and mine stone and ore near the castle, the goods are sold to its merchants for gold, and the gold is spent on more workers, new soldiers (up to 42 at a fully grown castle), castle upgrades (levels 1-4), new watchtowers, outposts on free islands, and war parties. The Map screen shows each faction's castle level, soldiers, workers, towers, outposts and gold, plus their latest move.
  • War: once a faction is strong, it sends war parties of 6-10 soldiers to retake a castle you conquered, or to march on your village. If attackers hold a conquered castle for 30 seconds with none of your side there to defend it, the castle goes back to its old owners and you must win it again.
  • Difficulty: enemies grow stronger as the days pass, as you level up, and with every faction you conquer (shown on the Map screen). Faction soldiers are tougher still. Taking on a faction or an island alone is very hard; bring an army.
  • Crafting stations are bigger: the furnace and the orc blast furnace take a 2 by 2 space, and the workbench and workshop are 2 blocks wide. Breaking any part breaks the whole station.
  • Furniture: chairs, stools, tables, desks, benches, barrels, cupboards and paintings are crafted at a workbench (planks, sticks, wool) and can be placed anywhere. They count as part of your base, so enemies do not spawn near them.
  • Screen layout (Settings): choose whether the hotbar, health, XP, mana and hunger bars and the info lines sit in the left or right corner, change their order, and resize them, with a live preview.
  • Worlds: small, medium and large now have a random number of islands (about 8-9, 10-12 and 13-15), and every size has every island type: the dragon's mountain, your home, two enemy factions, the goblin forest, the orc island, ice islands, and the Kraken's sea with a treasure island at the world's edge.
  • Orc island: rolling stone mountains to climb, and orc camps of every size - campfires (3-4 orcs), small huts (3-5 orcs, maybe an ogre), medium huts (5-7 orcs and an ogre) and big campsites (8-10 orcs and 1-2 ogres). Orcs come small with daggers, regular, big with warhammers, and with crossbows; ogres carry clubs. Stone towers at both ends have crossbow orcs on top and window slits.
  • The orc cave base: a shaft leads down from the tallest mountain into a great hall, two side chambers and a deep chamber 20-25 orcs and 5-6 ogres in all. The hall holds the orc blast furnace, the only place you can smelt mythril and adamantite bars. In the deepest chamber waits Grukk the Ogre King (4,500 health, two sledgehammers, twice an ogre's size) beside a treasure chest.
  • Goblin forest: goblin huts with 2-5 goblins (spears, daggers and bows), goblin archers in the lookout towers at both ends, and the Goblin Beastmaster's war camp guarded by 3-4 dagger goblins and 2-3 goblin archers. A captured villager waits to be rescued; once freed they head to your village to find work.
  • The Kraken's treasure is buried on a small sand island at the edge of the world: look for the red X and dig down. It stays sealed until the Kraken is slain. The Kraken roams under the water and rises to hunt you, and the sea around it is full of sunken ships.
  • Enemies now keep a little distance from each other instead of stacking on top of one another.
  • Multiplayer: the host's game runs every enemy and animal and spawns them around every player, so everyone fights the same monsters. Hits on shared enemies count for everyone; bosses share one health bar. Names show above players, and a line under the dragon distance shows each other player's direction and distance. Everyone sees each other mining. If the host's world is creative, everyone plays in creative (including flying).
  • Enemies never spawn within 5 blocks of your buildings, crafting stations, chests, beds, doors or torches.
  • Sunlight only reaches about 10 blocks below the surface. Once you have claimed your castle, the inside of buildings is always lit.
  • The dragon only regains health once every player has died and is at least 100 blocks from its lair.
  • Shops: every villager buys all the goods of their trade (the blacksmith ores, stones, bars, gems and metal gear; the herbalist flowers, seeds, crops and plants; the cook food; the fisher fish; the shipwright wood and wool; the beast trainer hides, bones and meat). Hold Buy or Sell to repeat it. You can give any of your own villagers a different job at any time. The miners' chest is handed over by the foreman.
  • Watchtowers have their door facing inland and window slits on every floor for archers and mages. The goblin forest has a wooden lookout at each end, manned by goblins.
  • Hunger (survival only): the hunger bar empties over about 5 to 6 minutes, twice as fast while sprinting. Eating fills it and heals you. Below 30 you stop healing on your own; at 0 you lose health until you eat.
  • Breeding: hold an animal's favorite food and press F near it (wheat for cows and sheep, carrots or pumpkins for boars, carrots for rabbits, apples or berries for deer, berries or salmon for bears, meat for wolves, fish for penguins and polar bears, seeds for birds). Two fed animals of the same kind close together have a baby, which grows up in 2 minutes.
  • Bag: the hotbar plus 6 rows of 9, and a gear locker for tools and weapons; extra items stay on the ground. Drag and drop between slots, drag an item out of the window (or onto Drop) to drop it, hover for a picture and stats, and press F with several things in reach to choose. Every tool and weapon needs sticks; any wood works; wood is the first tool tier. Cows and sheep give beef, mutton and wool. A second great ocean hides the Kraken and its sunken treasure (Kraken fang daggers: a permanent beastmaster bond).
  • Worlds are about twice the area of before (40% wider and 40% deeper). Oceans reach all the way down to the black stone layer, and every underwater edge is lined with sand at least 3 blocks thick, so the sea never floods caves. Backgrounds fade smoothly from one biome to the next.
  • Black stone: a 4-block layer between the normal underground and the magma realm. It cannot be broken from above, only from below (coming up from the magma realm).
  • The blacksmith sells every weapon and tool at every tier. Metal ones cost coins plus one bar of that metal (for example 1 gold bar for a gold pickaxe). Buying a better bow or staff keeps your old one.
  • Portals only appear on castle roofs, plus the wizard tower, magma fortress and the two realm arrival points.
  • Crafting has separate Weapons and Tools sections.
  • Factions: your faction is the abandoned castle you claim; the other two colors are enemies. Portals sit on castle roofs (and beside freed camps); click one to open the map and tap another purple portal to travel there with your army, for 15 to 50 coins.
  • Monster limits near you at once: about 6 to 10 night monsters on the surface (by world size), 6 underground, 4 frost skeletons on ice islands, 3 ghosts per burned village plus a rare ghoul, 3 roaming sky soldiers, 22 to 30 sea creatures (they appear within about 15 blocks of you at sea or on the shore), and 6 wild animals. Castles, camps and realm castles have their own fixed garrisons on top of that.
  • Blasts from cannons, trebuchets and blast arrows break blocks (75% drop as items), except in your own and allied villages. Fire arrows burn grass to ash, char trees and wooden buildings.
  • Test world: a normal medium world in save slot 4, with a God menu (give any item, upgrade any tool, weapon or armor, recruit soldiers, spawn any enemy or animal), super-speed flight (Fly button: beside Torch on phones, under ⋯ More or G on a computer) and a world speed button in the God menu (1x, 2x, 5x, 10x).
  • Ice islands are entirely snow, with small ice huts. Frost archers (their arrows slow you) and frost brutes (they shatter into ice) spawn around them and do not burn in sunlight.
  • Burned-village houses have open doorways you can walk through. Large caverns contain rock islands and pillars.
  • Penguins and polar bears can be gatherers: they bring back fish and meat.
  • Growth dust (herbalist, 160 coins): sprinkle on a crop, sapling, bush or cane to grow it a stage now and 50% faster afterwards.
  • A war engineer moves into your village after both the Sky Ranger and the Magma Knight are defeated, selling ballistas, cannons and trebuchets.
  • Mini-bosses fight in set patterns: wizards fire fans, rings and homing orbs and blink away from you; the Sky Ranger shoots triple arrows, rains arrows from above and leaps away; the Magma Knight and goblin beastmaster dash, slam and sweep.
  • Music keeps playing under menus; pausing plays a closing jingle and stops it. Typing in the crafting search box never triggers game keys; press Enter to leave it. Seeds plant on tilled soil with a normal click, and you can hold to plant a row.
  • Shops charge far more for crafted weapons, armor and tools (about 2.5 times, more at higher tiers), so building them yourself pays off. Melee weapons reach 5% farther with each tier.
  • Oceans are twice as deep, so digging under them is hard; sail instead.
  • Hold Shift to hold up a lit torch from your bag before placing it (Shift + click places it).
  • The herbalist only sells seeds and plants you have found, and miners can only be sent for ores you have found (coal and stone always).
  • Crafting: switch between all recipes and only the ones you can make right now.
  • Axes aim and work exactly like pickaxes, but only on trees, bamboo, cacti and mushroom trees (on mobile the joystick now targets the nearest tree part). Chopping any part of a tree brings the whole connected tree down, including pieces at ground level or under water.
  • Blacksmith upgrades: hold a tool, weapon or rod and talk to your blacksmith (or one in a conquered village) to upgrade it to the next tier. It costs 60% of the next tier's bars plus 40 coins per current tier.
  • Every tool can be crafted at a workbench, including the wooden pickaxe (6 planks, 2 sticks). The crafting screen has a search box and a Craft All button.
  • Bare hands can knock down trees, about 4 times slower than a wooden axe.
  • Tamed animals follow the same rules as soldiers: they reappear beside you if more than 15 blocks away or stuck, wait on shore when you sail, and rejoin when you land.
  • Hide items hides the Bag, Craft, Army and Map buttons; your hotbar stays. Save is in the Menu (or press K on a keyboard). Full screen is only offered on computers.
  • Tiers: every tool, weapon and armor line goes wood (or the starter), iron, gold, diamond, mythril, adamantite. Adamantite armor makes you immune to lava.
  • Lava flows and settles like a liquid, slows you down and burns. The bottom 3 to 5 rows of the world are unbreakable black bedrock with sealed lava pockets.
  • Underground water only sits in small sealed stone pockets, no bigger than 10 x 10.
  • Enemy villages have their own recruiter, quartermaster and beast trainer, who work for you once you conquer the village. Warlords earn gold and buy more troops over time (up to 20).
  • Travel: from the map you can travel with your army to the fishing village, your fishermen, and any enemy village you have conquered.
  • Magma realm soldiers have red skin and are immune to fire. Fire ferns and ember blooms grow there.
  • Crafting is a tab in the bag (the Bag button, then Crafting, or C on a keyboard). Hover over an item in your bag to see what it is. Music turns tense when enemies fight you.
  • Enemy castles hold about 15 soldiers led by a Warlord (450 hp). He orders charges when you approach, sounds the retreat when badly hurt, and sends raiding parties.
  • Beastmaster: each tamed companion (up to 3) raises every ally's attack by 5% (15% max).
  • Bows: arrows fly faster and straighter. Adamantite bow arrows home in on enemies. Fire bow arrows burn, blast bow arrows explode, and the tri-shot bow fires three at once.
  • Staffs: adamantite staff bolts home in. The storm staff's lightning chains to 2 more enemies, the ward staff gives you and nearby allies a shield for 12 seconds, and the ember staff sets enemies on fire.
  • Trees only grow on dirt and grass. Ice is slippery. Sound and music can be turned off in Settings.
  • Water washes away plants, crops and torches (saplings, bushes, herbs and torches drop as items). Mushroom trees, crystals and tree trunks stay in place as water flows past; chop them with an axe.
  • Fast travel: open the map and choose Travel to a fisherman to jump straight to the fishing village or any of your fishermen with your party.
  • Tools: pickaxes break blocks and mushrooms only; axes chop trees, bamboo, cacti and mushroom trees; hoes till soil, clear plants and harvest crops; hammers break back walls and reshape blocks. Every tool has wood, iron, gold, diamond, mythril and adamantite tiers, and each tier is faster.
  • Farming: till grass or dirt with a hoe, plant wheat, carrot or pumpkin seeds (from the herbalist or tall grass), and harvest ripe crops with the hoe.
  • Food and healing potions share a 5 second cooldown.
  • Save slots: choose slot 1, 2 or 3 for each new world. Slot 1 is also saved to your account.
  • Computer: hold Shift while moving to sprint 20% faster; Shift + click places a torch; Full screen (under ⋯ More) enlarges the game.
  • Zombies and skeletons burn in daylight. Wolves only attack if provoked, animals fight back against whoever hurt them, and no animals spawn inside your villages.
  • Beds: craft one (10 planks, 2 herbs) and sleep at night to skip to morning. It becomes your respawn point.
  • Sky wings (sky chests only): hold jump in the air to fly for 10 seconds (sky feathers raise it); they recharge on the ground. Sky chests stay sealed until the Sky Ranger is defeated.
  • Soldiers move as fast as you and heal when out of combat. Fallen soldiers are gone for good, but a reserve soldier from your garrison takes their place and comes to you.
  • Fishers can be assigned as a villager job. On the map, choose whether each shopkeeper works at home or in a conquered village. The mine foreman lets you pick the ore for each miner.
  • Your army can hold up to 20 soldiers. Teammates who fall far behind or get stuck reappear next to you once you are on land.
  • Only trees in the forest cast shade when packed together.
  • Delete and Drop: in your bag, pick up an item and tap Delete twice to destroy it, or tap Drop (or drag it out of the bag) to put it on the ground.
  • Fire weapons (magma staff, magma blade) keep burning enemies for 3 seconds.
  • Shaping: craft stairs, slopes and half blocks, or reshape placed blocks with the shaping hammer.
  • Miners: in the miner hut, choose which ore they dig. They fill a pack of 8 and pop back up to drop it in the hut chest.
  • Home helpers: the Quartermaster upgrades soldiers, the Beast trainer upgrades tamed animals, and the Recruiter hires new soldiers. New shop villagers get a new house if every house is taken.
  • Caves are pitch black without a light: hold a torch, place torches, or wear a lantern. Ground below the surface gets darker quickly unless lit.
  • The Quartermaster in your castle sells permanent upgrades for all your soldiers: armor, weapons and training, 5 levels each.
  • Miners build a hut over a mine shaft, go down to dig and carry ore back up to the hut chest.
  • Only you and your allies can open doors in your village. Enemies can open doors in their own castles.
  • Hold Ctrl and scroll, or pinch, to zoom the view. Zoom all the way out to open the map.
  • Day lasts 5 minutes, night 2 minutes. Monsters never spawn in lit areas or inside buildings.
  • Falls of more than 11 blocks hurt. Landing in water or wearing the feather charm prevents it.
  • Water levels itself out, washes away mushrooms and moss, and turns lava into obsidian.
  • Each day there is a 1 in 5 chance of an enemy raid on the surface (can be turned off) and a 1 in 4 chance a new villager arrives at your home.
  • Respawning: you, your party and your pets come back at your bed or home with full health and a full hunger bar.
  • Fence pens: any straight line of 4 or more fences on the ground is a pen. Farm animals stay inside, cannot be hurt and produce goods each morning (see Wildlife). Fences are drawn in front of you.
  • Giving items (multiplayer): stand within 5 blocks of a friend and press F, or use Give in your bag.
  • Ending: slaying Vorthax plays an epilogue and credits. Keep playing, or start New Game Plus with your character: a new world where enemies are 50% tougher for each New Game Plus.
  • Enemy ships: a war party's ship has 260 (rowboat), 650 (longship) or 1,200 (warship) health, more as enemies grow stronger. Sunk crews drown after 45 seconds in the sea far from land.
  • Patrol ships hunt your boat and keep about 9 blocks away while their archers, mages and cannons fire. They head home if you sail more than 90 blocks away.
  • Your soldiers aboard your boat also shoot at enemy ships, not only at sea creatures.
  • Frozen sea: sea creatures stay under the ice; projectiles still fly over it. Boats cannot be launched while the sea is frozen.
  • Slowed enemies (frost runes, frost arrows) move at half speed for a moment.
  • Guests in multiplayer save to slot 5 (Friend's world). Loading it plays it as your own world; host it again from Multiplayer, then Host a saved world.
  • Village growth, loyalty, tribute and expeditions are run by the host in multiplayer; only the host can grow the village.
  • Enemy workers open the doors of their own village; builders only work on one project at a time per village.
  • Woodcutters plant a sapling where they fell a tree, so the forest around an enemy village slowly regrows near you.
  • Charms: use one to wear it; only one is worn at a time, and wearing another gives the first back.
  • Surrender: if you kill a faction's warlord while you stand in their base and no more than a tenth of their soldiers (compared with the most they had) are left, the rest surrender and join your army.
  • Coins from monsters: a monster killed by an enemy faction pays its coins to that faction, unless you hit it at least once.
  • Villagers hide in their castle while raiders are in the village, and come back out 15 seconds after the last raider leaves or falls.
  • Village workers: woodcutters fell trees near your village (and plant saplings), farmers harvest ripe crops and replant; both carry their loads to the chest in your castle. When you are far away, each brings a day's share every morning.
  • Expeditions come back on foot to the Recruiter; talk to him to collect the loot.
  • Boss castles: rooms in the sky castle and the magma keep stay dark until you step into them. They stay dark for everyone until someone enters.
  • The dragon's gold heap: walk into it to scoop up gold (5 to 9 coins each time, sometimes a gold bar); the heap slides down as you take from it.
  • Rain fills hollows on the surface and drains away after the rain stops; snow layers melt outside winter. You can build straight over snow.
  • Arrow slits are solid to walk into, but arrows, bolts and spells fly through them.
  • Storm ore forms only where lightning strikes open dirt or grass in a storm, or around a lightning rod.
  • A faction surrenders once its warlord is dead and no more than a quarter of its people are left; you choose to take its soldiers into your army or execute them.
  • Rain water dries up by the next morning.
  • Test world tools: the test world (save slot 4) starts with a prepped farm a short walk from where you begin: 16 crop plots, a fenced pen (no gate) with 2 cows, 2 sheep, a boar and a rabbit inside, and a farmhouse with a bed, workbench, furnace and a chest of seeds and food. The God menu's World tab builds another farm next to you, ripens nearby crops, skips to the next morning, takes you to the test farm, forces the weather (natural, clear, rain, thunderstorm, snowstorm) and its strength (light, medium, strong), and starts a flash flood, a lightning strike or dries up rain water at once. Forced weather lasts until you pick Natural. None of this exists in other worlds.
  • Mist clouds float low over the islands, drift slowly with the wind for about 80 blocks, then fade out and drift in again from where they started. You can see the sky through them and you walk behind them.
  • Water and plants: rain puddles, flash floods and flowing water fill the inside of fences and cover grass, flowers, crops, bushes and mushrooms. The plants stay where they are under the water and are back once it dries; they don't grow while covered. Torches, lanterns, lamps and campfires that water reaches are knocked loose and drop as items.
  • The cold: on ice islands all year, and on every island in winter, grass plants, flowers, crops and saplings out in the open die and a snow layer takes their place. Snow builds up there and doesn't melt (on ice islands it never melts; elsewhere it melts after winter). Rain over an ice island falls as snow. Plants under a roof survive. In spring and summer, tall grass, ferns and flowers slowly sprout again on bare grass; crops must be replanted.
  • Farm crafting: the Farm tab in crafting holds every seed and plant (sapling, tall grass, fern, bush, red, yellow and blue flowers, berry bush, herb plant, sugar cane, cactus, glow mushroom), fences, the jack-o'-lantern and shears. All plant recipes are made by hand. Plants must be set on solid ground. Fences stacked on top of each other join into one tall post.
  • Slopes: natural slopes look like the normal block cut on a slant (a grass slope keeps its grass top along the slant). Plants, grass and crops never grow on a slope, slab or stair, and you can't plant them there; any already there are cleared when the world loads.
  • Walking on slopes: players, soldiers and animals glide smoothly up and down slopes and stairs and stay on the ground going downhill, instead of hopping from step to step. One-block steps are still climbed by walking into them.
  • Doorways: when a villager you hire gets a new house, when the miner's hut is built, or when you place a building, the ground outside each door is reshaped for at least 5 blocks. Hills are cut away and dips filled, with a slope rising or falling one block per block to the natural ground, so you can walk straight out. Trees in the way are removed, and a ramp stops at the next building.
  • Realm castles: the Sky Ranger's castle is a tall tower of five floors (entrance hall and armory, library, two barracks, sky garden, and the throne hall on top), climbed through gaps in each floor with ledges under them. The Magma Knight's keep is a wide maze of 18 small rooms on three levels (upper halls, main halls and a dungeon with lava pits and cages), joined at random by doorways and holes, with dead ends; his throne room is entered from the upper level and climbed down by ledges. Rooms you haven't entered are hidden in thick fog, white in the sky tower and red in the magma keep, with faint outlines showing. Stepping into a room uncovers it for good, and the guards inside only wake when their room is uncovered or when they are hit.
  • Sky height: in worlds made from Beta 0.9 on, the sky realm is taller, leaving about 29 rows of open air above the Sky Ranger's tower so you can see all of it and climb or build over the top. Older worlds keep their sky height; their tower is a little squatter (floors 8 blocks apart) and has about 15 rows of sky above it.
  • Falling trees: a chopped tree tips away from you until any part of it touches a solid block (the ground, a hill or a wall), then breaks into tumbling pieces of wood and leaves right there; nothing passes through the ground. Leaves are always drawn in front of the wood, while standing, falling and breaking apart.
  • Taming reach: with a taming whistle (or as a Beastmaster) you can tame an animal from about 3 blocks from its edge, even while it runs; flying birds can be tamed from about 4.5 blocks.
  • Lanterns: a lantern must hang from something: a ceiling above it, a wall beside it, the back wall of a building, or a built block under it. Lanterns can't be placed out in the open. The village hangs its lanterns on the outside walls of its houses beside the doors.
  • Level villages: in your village and every faction village, the ground between neighbouring buildings is flat at floor level (hills cut away, dips and cave mouths filled, trees removed). Outside the two end buildings there are 3 flat blocks, then a slope up or down to the natural ground. This is redone when you claim your village, when a house or the miner's hut is built, and when a faction finishes a new house.
  • Grappling hooks: a permanent upgrade (worn like an accessory) in three tiers: grappling hook (10 blocks), iron grappling hook (16) and mythril grappling hook (24); better hooks also pull faster. Hold X, or the Hook joystick near the right stick on phones (drag it to aim), to wind up, and let go to toss it toward where you aim, like a fishing cast: it flies in an arc on a loose line, so aim a little high. The rope pays out loose behind the hook and lies on the ground where it falls. If the hook lands on ground at or below your feet it just lies there until you press the hook key again to reel it in; if it hits a wall, a ceiling or a ledge above you it grabs and pulls you to it; if that is near the top of a block with room above, you climb onto it. It holds you where it pulled you until you press the hook key again; then you climb onto the ledge if there is one, or drop. Craft at a workbench (3 iron bars + 3 wool; 4 gold bars + 4 wool; 4 mythril bars + 4 wool), find in chests, or pick '+ Grappling hook' as your starting items.
  • Phone torch: the Torch button lights your way; while it is on, one tap places a torch where you tap and tools rest (weapons still swing). With it off, double-tap a spot to place a torch.
  • The eye (under ⋯ More, or H) lists what shows on screen, all ticked to start: untick the item bar, bars, info, mini-map or a button to hide it at once.
  • The top bar keeps Bag (with Crafting as a tab), Army, Buildings, the God menu in the test world, Menu and ⋯ More (Full screen, Hide items, Quick save, Show the mini-map, Hide the buttons).
  • Settings > Torches: choose what a tap does with the Torch button on, whether double-tap places a torch, whether tools rest while the torch is on, and whether holding Shift lights your torch.
  • Phones, Stick and tap: mining always takes the nearest block toward your tap.
  • Miners: an enemy hit never kills a miner; he runs straight back to the village, rests, then returns to the mine. Miners walk along their tunnels and climb the shaft smoothly.
  • Felling trees: only the leaves of the tree you cut come down; where crowns touch, each leaf belongs to its nearest trunk.
  • Baby animals: young animals have their own look (calf, lamb, striped piglet, chick, bunny, spotted fawn, bear and polar bear cubs, penguin chick, wolf pup) and grow into adults after a couple of minutes.
  • Lava flows like water, filling in levels and spreading out until it is level, but slowly, creeping about a block every quarter second. You can swim in it slowly (hold jump to rise). It burns plants, wood, fences, furniture and leaves it touches, scorches grass to dirt, destroys items (not coins) that fall into it, and hardens to obsidian or stone where it meets water. Wading in lava slows you like honey and burns you unless your armor protects you.
  • Fast travel: when you use a portal, the map has a Go home button that takes you straight to your home village for the usual fee.
  • Capturing a village: once its warlord is dead and no more than a quarter of the most people its island has held are left on that island, the faction surrenders and you choose to take or execute the soldiers there. Its people away from the island (war parties, scouts, ships) don't count, and you don't need to be there. Enemy castles have no guards on their roofs.
  • Lava pools: small pools of lava, 2 to 6 blocks across, sometimes sit on the surface (away from villages and water) and in the rock underground.
  • Buckets: craft a bucket from 3 iron bars at a workbench. Use it on water to fill it; use the water bucket to pour the water where you aim. Water poured on lava turns it to obsidian or stone.
  • Lava: jump while your head is out of the lava to climb out onto the shore. You can place blocks in and over lava to bridge it.
  • Trees: when a tree falls, its logs drop from the rubble where it lands; walk over them to pick them up.
  • Grappling hook: while it holds you, press jump to let go and jump off.
  • Doors: tap an open door to close it, even with water flowing through it; it holds the water back.
  • Thrown weapons: the Adamantite sword and the Magma blade fly out and come back like a boomerang when you attack. The Adamantite mace is on a chain: tap to throw it, hold to spin it above your head, let go to throw it where you aim. The Adamantite and Kraken daggers are thrown one after another, up to 3 at once.
  • Desktop: install the game from pumped60.github.io/dragon-amulet-play in Chrome or Edge, or download the Mac and Windows apps from github.com/pumped60/dragon-amulet-play/releases/latest.
  • Fire: wood, leaves, plants and grass burn with flames, spread a little and burn out to charred wood, ash or nothing; water and rain put fires out.
  • Saplings: every kind of tree drops and grows from its own sapling.
  • Phones: joysticks by default; "Stick and tap" puts the move stick on the left (Settings > Movement stick side moves it to the right for left-handed players) and a tap anywhere mines, builds or attacks there. You always sprint while moving (Settings can turn it off), and a double-tap places a torch.
  • The Multiplayer menu lists your recent shared worlds with their codes: Host again reopens a world you hosted with the same code; Join rejoins a friend.
  • Shop prices: anything that can be crafted costs half of what its materials are worth (a material is worth 5 times its sell value). Selling never pays more than half the shop price.
  • Pickaxes also clear grass, flowers, plants and leaves.
  • A double jump breaks a fall: fall damage counts from where you double jumped.
  • Weather can be switched off: choose Off when making a world, or Settings > Weather: Off.
  • Phones: the move stick is on the left; Settings > Movement stick side puts it on the right for left-handed players.
  • Dragonborn on a computer: hold B to breathe fire where the mouse points. The flames stop at the ground and walls.
  • Silver gear sits between wood and iron in every weapon and tool line (sword, mace, daggers, bow, staff, pickaxe, axe, hoe, hammer, fishing rod), halfway between the two. It is made from silver bars, smelted from silver ore at a furnace.
  • Trees: trunks run up inside the crown and show through the leaves; big trees have branches with leafy clumps; spruces are tall cones.
  • In a shared world everything matches: a tree cut by anyone falls for everyone, back walls and new village houses arrive whole, and a wanderer given a job by any player becomes the same villager for all. Only the host builds houses; a guest's choice is sent to the host.
  • Water falls through drop-through platforms, slides down slopes and stairs, and is drawn in front of furniture standing in it. Rain and flash floods leave half as much water as they used to.
  • Penned animals stay in their pen until flood water is three quarters of a block above the top of the fence.
  • On the surface, a space is dark only when it is closed in on every side and has a back wall; a roof alone doesn't darken it.
  • Storms and rain last 12 hours of game time (the first half of the day); their water drains away 6 hours later.
  • Mouse aim lock is on by default; L or Settings turns it off.
  • Every menu has an X in its top right corner that stays visible however far you scroll. In the new world screen every class button is the same size, with the Dragonborne weapon tally on a line under them.
  • The Dragonborn: a sixth class, always playable in the test world and unlocked for new games by defeating Vorthax in survival. He flies whenever he likes and breathes fire (hold B, or the Breath button on phones), which uses mana. Vorthax drops the dragonborne weapon of your class; a new Dragonborn starts with every one unlocked.
  • Bombs: small and fire bombs are crafted at a workbench from black powder (2 coal and a bone); big and sticky bombs come from the Demolitionist, who arrives after the Goblin Beastmaster is beaten. Bombs break blocks; rubble crumbles away after 3 days. Your own bombs hurt you a little and never break your own village.
  • Shops: talk to a shopkeeper to upgrade their shop (levels 1 to 3, coins and building materials). Level 2 needs a Town and level 3 a City; each level sells better goods.
  • Bosses, in order: Goblin Beastmaster (bring 3 soldiers), Archmage (4), Magma Wizard (5), Sky Ranger (6), Magma Knight (8), Grukk (10), Kraken (12), Vorthax (20). With fewer, your blows barely hurt and the boss heals. The wizards fly and chase you; the Beastmaster vanishes and backstabs; a red mark means the Sky Ranger is about to shoot that fighter; when the Magma Knight glows, hit it with four fighters to crack its armor.
  • Trophies: a boss trophy is a 2 by 2 replica of the boss. Stand it on two trophy shelves side by side; shelves hang on a back wall.
  • Armies: you need your whole army (20 soldiers) to beat Vorthax; alone or with a small band the dragon shrugs off your blows and heals. Enemy villages and outposts gang up on a lone attacker, so bring at least 5 soldiers; if you die there, the garrison heals.
  • Runes: each rune has levels I to V. Set more of the same rune into a weapon to level it up; level II needs 2 more runes, III 3, IV 4 and V 5. A different rune starts again at level I.
  • Phones: the game fills the whole screen on the first tap, and the grappling hook has its own small aiming joystick just left of the right stick: drag to aim, let go to throw.
